# MALLAM TOWN — Illustration Brief

### JHS edition · by Lecturer Sam
### A commissioning document for the artist

---

## 1. What this is

This brief commissions **fifteen black-and-white line drawings** — one opening each chapter — plus a **cover**, a **character design sheet**, and the **map** (already drawn; see `Mallam-Town-Map.svg`).

Read the whole of this document before you draw anything. Sections 3 and 4 are **requirements**, not preferences. If a drawing breaks one of them it will be rejected, however good it looks.

Every quotation in this brief is taken verbatim from the manuscript. Where a detail is specified — *"a dent in the rear passenger door"*, *"a blue Biro that skipped"* — it is in the text and it matters.

## 3. Style specification

**Medium.** Black-and-white line art. Pen and ink, or clean digital line. No grey tone, no shading gradients, no cross-hatching denser than about 30%. Solid black is allowed and encouraged for a single focal shape per drawing.

**Why line art.** This book will be printed on the paper Ghanaian textbooks are printed on, and photocopied in schools for years afterwards. Line art survives that. Tonal work turns to mud on the third photocopy.

**Line weight.** Two weights only, plus solid black:
- **0.5 pt** — interior detail, faces, hands, cloth folds
- **1.2 pt** — outlines and the silhouette that carries the image
- **Solid black** — one element per drawing, maximum

At the printed size (see §9) anything below 0.4 pt will drop out. Test your line weights by printing your artwork at 60% and looking at it across a room.

**Register.** Observed, not dramatised. These are pictures of a real town: concrete, dust, zinc, washing lines, pot lids, crates, kiosks. Draw the ordinary detail accurately and the drama will look after itself.

**What the book is not.** It is not an action thriller. It is not horror. It is a book about a town that learns to look at what is in front of it. If a drawing could sit on the cover of a thriller, it is the wrong drawing.

---

## 4. Hard rules

These are not negotiable.

### 4.1 Nene: no masks, no skulls, no shadows, no eeriness

Nene is a **traditional herbalist** — a healer who treats people with plants. She is drawn as exactly what she is: **an old woman of seventy-one with bad knees, a stick, and a basket.**

**Do not draw:** masks, skulls, bones, calabashes of mysterious contents, feathers, beads arranged as regalia, smoke, incense, ritual objects, altars, shrines, shadows falling across her face, glowing eyes, hovering birds, or anything that suggests the supernatural.

**Draw instead:** the mortar worn smooth on one side. The shelf of small bottles with names scratched into the glass. Bundles of herbs hanging from a line across the ceiling. The stick. The knees that hurt.

Her room is a **workroom**, not a temple. It smells of bark. There is a pot on a single gas stove.

> The test: if a drawing of Nene would look at home in a horror comic, it has failed.

### 4.2 The crash is not drawn

Chapter 15 contains a serious injury. It is described by sound, not by sight:

> *"The sound was not a scream. It was a noise Kwame had heard once, years ago, at the rank, and had never managed to forget: the ordinary, muffled sound of a body in the way of something heavy."*

**Do not draw the crash, the body, blood, or the injury.** The Chapter 15 drawing is the gate (see §6.15). If at any point you feel the book wants a picture of the crash — it does not. The restraint is the point.

### 4.3 Nobody touches Sarpong

> *"That not one person in a hundred put out a hand. No shout. No stone. Nothing at all."*

There is no mob in this book. Where Sarpong appears, he is alone in the frame or leaving it. **No raised fists, no crowd closing in, no hands on him.**

### 4.4 The pistol

A woman holds a pistol in Chapter 3. The text is precise:

> *"The woman held a pistol low against her thigh, almost casual, like a handbag."*

Draw it **small, low, and casual** — held against the thigh, not raised, not pointed at the reader, not firing. If you can suggest it with a shape at her hip and no clear outline, do that. **Never draw it pointed at anyone's face.** Never draw a muzzle flash.

### 4.5 Mansa is unhurt

Mansa is held in a locked room and is **never struck**. There are no bruises, no torn clothing, no ropes, no suggestion of what did not happen. She is drawn **whole, dressed, and dignified** throughout. The cruelty done to her in this story is that the town assumed otherwise. Do not undermine that by drawing her as a victim.

### 4.6 No guns on the cover, no running man

See §7.

## 5. Character design sheet — deliver this first

Eight figures on one page, full length, side by side, in a neutral row. Young readers track characters by **silhouette**, and consistency is the single thing that makes an illustrated reader feel professional.

| Character | Age | Build & dress | The one thing you must get right |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Kwame Asante** | middle age | Medium build. Short-sleeve shirt, trousers. Carries a rag. | **His hands.** Unmarked, open, palms often turned outward. He is a driver and a peacemaker; his hands are how he does both. |
| **Mansa Asante** | 38 | "*Thirty-eight and deliberate in every motion.*" Wears a wrapper — the blue one with the Sunday pattern. | **Deliberateness.** She is never drawn hurrying or dishevelled. Even frightened, she is composed. |
| **Nene** | 71 | Short, solid. Cloth with pockets. Head tie. | **The stick and the basket.** Plus the knees — she leans, and getting down is work. See §4.1. |
| **Chief Inspector Nanfuri** | 40s | Uniform. "*Tired in the unremarkable way of a man with too many open files.*" | **The shoulders.** The text reads him twice by *"the set of the shoulders."* |
| **Inspector Adzo Mensah** | ~30 | Uniform, "*hands at her sides and her cap under one arm.*" | **The cap under the arm.** She carries a folder. She is young for her rank; draw her composed, never unsure. |
| **Kwabena Dapaah** | 54 | "*Built like the thing he worked with — solid, square, slow.*" | **The hands.** Big, square, often opened palm-up on his knees. He comes to Nene *about his hands*. |
| **Yaw Sarpong** | 56 | "*Fifty-six and well fed.*" | **Ordinariness.** He looks like a landlord arriving to deal with a small problem. Not a villain's face. |
| **Old Woman on the veranda** | elderly | A chair on a veranda. | **She is a witness.** She is drawn watching. |

**Also needed on the sheet:** Kwame's taxi (yellow, faded, **a dent in the rear passenger door**), the blue container at the rank, Nene's mortar and shelf of bottles, the compound gate (rusted, crooked at the top, one post bent beyond repair).

## 6. The fifteen drawings

---

### 6.1 Chapter One — The Peacemaker

**The moment.** Dawn at the rank. Kwame sitting on an overturned plastic crate beside his taxi, polishing the side mirror.

> *"Kwame Asante sat on an overturned plastic crate beside his own taxi, polishing his side mirror with a rag."*

**Composition.** Kwame in the left third, low, seated — the crate puts him below the car. The taxi fills the right. Rank behind him in half-light: other taxis, a kiosk, a crate or two. **Give the side mirror a clear, readable shape** — the reader must remember it, because it comes back in Chapter 12 and again in Chapter 14.

**Mood.** Ordinary, unhurried, faintly proud. This is a man at peace with his work, eleven days before it is taken from him.

**Do not draw:** the preacher (he is in the scene but not the picture), Mansa, or anything that foreshadows.

---

### 6.2 Chapter Two — The Empty Room

**The moment.** Kwame standing in the doorway of their room in the dark, holding his phone out, its weak light falling on nothing.

> *"He thumbed the screen alive and swept its weak light across the room. Empty bed. Empty corner. The plastic chair, empty."*

**Composition.** The doorway framing the room. Kwame a silhouette against a little light from the yard. The phone is the only light source — a weak pool of it, and the emptiness beyond. **The perfume is not drawable.** Do not put a perfume bottle on the bedside table; the point is that she is *smelled and not found*. Draw the absence.

**Do not draw:** Mansa, a struggle, a broken window, an open gate.

---

### 6.3 Chapter Three — Dangerous Lane

**The moment.** The taxi stopped on Dangerous Lane, a car swung across the lane ahead of it, headlights crowding in behind. Three figures getting out. Unhurried.

> *"Two men and a woman got out of the car in front. Unhurried. That was the thing he would remember afterwards — how unhurried they were."*

**Composition.** Night. The taxi caught between two vehicles. **Keep the three figures calm** — walking, not running; one carries a tyre iron; the woman's pistol low against her thigh (§4.4). Kwame visible through the windscreen with his hands still on the wheel. Bush close on both sides; the streetlights have given up.

**Do not draw:** the beating. No blows, no blood, no faces contorted. The violence of this chapter is in the reader's imagination and must stay there.

---

### 6.4 Chapter Four — Nene

**The moment.** Before sunrise. Nene kneeling in the gutter on Dangerous Lane, having just turned Kwame over by the good arm. Her basket set down beside her. A few feet away, face-down in the dust, a phone.

> *"There was a shape in the gutter on Dangerous Lane."*
>
> *"She set the basket down. It took her a while to kneel, and it hurt, and she said so to nobody."*

**Composition.** **Low viewpoint** — we are down in the gutter with them. Nene is the only upright thing. The road is "*the colour of nothing yet.*" **Put the phone clearly in the frame, a few feet away, face-down in the dust** — it is the single most important object in the book after the mirror, and it must be planted here to pay off in Chapters 7, 10 and 13.

**Mood.** Not mysterious, not mystical. Competent. An old woman doing work she knows how to do, at an hour when nobody is watching.

**Do not draw:** anything from §4.1. No light from her hands. No leaves glowing.

---

### 6.5 Chapter Five — The Report

**The moment.** Kwame leaning on the counter at the police post. The Desk Officer's ledger and blue Biro. Through a half-open office door behind the counter, the senior officer, half-seen.

> *"The Desk Officer did not look up more than was necessary. He had a thick ledger open in front of him and a blue Biro that skipped."*
> *"Behind the counter, through a half-open office door, Kwame caught a glimpse of the senior officer."*

**Composition.** Counter as a strong horizontal across the frame. Kwame leaning on it, favouring his left side. The ledger open; **the Biro must read clearly** — it is the instrument that writes down the one fact that will eventually clear him. Nanfuri **half-visible only**: a shoulder, an arm, a man on a phone. He is not yet a character to the reader.

**Do not draw:** a police station that looks threatening. This one is merely tired — a ceiling fan turning too slowly to matter.

---

### 6.6 Chapter Six — A Face on the Radio

**The moment.** Kwame setting the pestle down on the table very, very carefully. The radio on the shelf behind him.

> *"…the thing that made Kwame set the pestle down on the table very carefully, as though it were made of something that would break."*

**Composition.** **Draw the hands and the pestle** — the whole image is in the care with which he puts it down. Kwame seated, very still, not looking at the radio. The radio on the shelf between a tin and a bottle with a name scratched into the glass. Nene somewhere in the frame, turned away, not watching him.

**Mood.** Stillness. A man hearing his own name on the radio and not moving.

**Do not draw:** Kwame's face in anguish, a wanted poster, a radio microphone, or a crowd listening.

---

### 6.7 Chapter Seven — What Nene Knows

**The moment.** **Two small bottles on a shelf, with two boys' names scratched into the glass.** Nothing else.

> *"There was a mortar in the corner, worn smooth on one side, and a shelf of small bottles, each with a name scratched into the glass."*

**Composition.** A close, quiet still life. Two bottles in the foreground, sharp and readable: **KWESI** and **EKOW**, scratched into the glass. The rest of the shelf going soft behind them. This is the only drawing in the book with no people in it, and it should be the one that stops the reader longest.

**Do not draw:** the boys. They are never seen in this book. Their absence is the point.

---

### 6.8 Chapter Eight — Mansa

**The moment.** Mansa on the mat in the locked room, the bright square of window-light on the wall beside her, her head turned toward the wall.

> *"The room had one window and the window had no glass, and the light coming through it made a bright square on the wall. Mansa watched the square."*

**Composition.** **The square of light is the clock and the cage** — make it the brightest thing in the frame. Mansa seated on the mat, composed, **whole and unhurt** (§4.5). A bucket in the corner. The door opening outwards — draw the hinges on the outside if you can, so the reader sees it locks from without.

**Mood.** Not terror. Endurance, and a mind still working.

**Do not draw:** chains, ropes, bruises, torn clothing, or a man in the frame. Nothing happened to her in this room, and the drawing must say so.

---

### 6.9 Chapter Nine — What Mansa Saw

**The moment.** Flashback. Mansa at the fence in the dark, watching men unload a truck by torchlight behind the uncompleted houses. Silhouettes only.

> *"…she had walked as far as the uncompleted houses and had seen men unloading a truck in the dark with torches, and had known one of the faces."*

**Composition.** Mansa in the near foreground, small, half-hidden, seen from behind or in profile at the edge of the frame. The men **silhouettes only** — no faces readable except that one of them must be *familiar in shape*. The uncompleted houses behind: rebar and roofless blocks. Torchlight, not floodlight.

**Mood.** This is the hinge of the whole book. Make the reader lean in.

**Do not draw:** what is in the truck.

---

### 6.10 Chapter Ten — The Plan

**The moment.** Nene at the door with her basket, leaving. Kwame behind her.

> *"Lock it behind me," she said.*

**Composition.** Nene going out through the doorway — **she is the one who moves in this chapter; Kwame stays.** Basket on her arm, stick in hand, already turned away. Kwame in the room behind, still, watching her go. The doorway frames her.

**Mood.** Purpose. She has decided something and is not going to discuss it.

**Do not draw:** a map, a plan on paper, a diagram, or anything that suggests conspiracy. The plan is a woman going to market.

---

### 6.11 Chapter Eleven — The Carpenter

**The moment.** Kwabena Dapaah frozen in the doorway, having just seen Kwame in the half-dark behind. Knuckles white on the handle of his basket.

> *"His knuckles went white on the handle of the basket and he did not move and he did not speak."*
>
> And then, quietly, almost to himself: *"You're the husband."*

**Composition.** **Draw the hands on the basket handle** — that is where the whole chapter lives. The text holds him there for five full seconds while the yard goes on being a yard. Kwabena in the doorway, square and solid, stopped dead. Kwame half-seen in the room behind, in shadow, rising. The unopened bowl of medicine on the wall beside him.

**Mood.** A man caught. Five seconds, held still.

**Do not draw:** the door in the wall (that is Chapter 14), or a workshop.

---

### 6.12 Chapter Twelve — The Narrow Escape

**The moment.** Nene at the mouth of the alley at dusk, stick under one arm, the whole compound behind her, filling the alley with phone lights.

> *"Somebody held up a phone light, and then somebody else did, and the alley filled with small white lights."*

**Composition.** **Choose this over Kwame running.** Nene small in the foreground, the crowd massed behind her — and the crowd must be **faces turned toward the reader**, not backs of heads. These are people who have decided to be seen. Little white phone lights held up. The two men in the middle distance, leaving.

**Mood.** Not triumph. A town deciding to look.

**Do not draw:** torches (the text is specific — *phone* lights), mobs, raised fists, or anyone laying hands on the men (§4.3).

---

### 6.13 Chapter Thirteen — The Honest Inspector

**The moment.** Night. Inspector Adzo Mensah at Nene's door with her cap under one arm. Nene standing aside to let her in. Two women, one doorway.

> *"Inspector Adzo Mensah," the woman said. "Mallam Town Police Post."*
>
> And then: *"I am not here to arrest anyone. May I come in?"*

**Composition.** The doorway as a vertical dividing the frame. **The two women should be nearly the same height in the frame** — neither towering. Mensah in uniform, cap under her arm, folder in hand, standing on the threshold and *not coming in* until invited. Nene inside, stepping aside. Kwame somewhere behind, half in shadow.

**Mood.** Two kinds of authority meeting, carefully. Neither woman gives ground; neither needs to.

**Do not draw:** a police car, handcuffs, a torch beam in the face, or a weapon.

---

### 6.14 Chapter Fourteen — Dawn at the Uncompleted House

**The moment.** Kwabena Dapaah's hands fitting a chisel into the seam of the wall. The door about to open. The crowd behind him. Dawn light coming in.

> *"He took out a chisel, fitted it into the seam, and pressed. The door opened."*

**Composition.** **Hands and chisel in the foreground, sharp.** Kwabena's back. The wall filling most of the frame, with the seam a hairline — the reader should have to look for it. Behind him, the crowd: the two mothers, Ama from the kiosk, the food-stall woman with her pot, Papa Kofi, the Old Woman. **Dawn light raking across from one side.**

**Mood.** Daylight as an event. The book's turning point, and it happens in the open.

**Do not draw:** Mansa in the doorway (she is found a beat later and that reveal belongs to the text), or the interior of the room.

---

### 6.15 Chapter Fifteen — The Gate

**The moment.** Kwame with his hand flat on the rusted compound gate, pushing it open. The rank waking behind him.

> *"Then he pushed, and it screamed — a long metal shriek that went the whole length of the rank and out into the road, loud and rusty and impossible to ignore."*
> *"This time, everybody heard."*

**Composition.** Kwame from behind or in three-quarter, one hand flat on the gate, pushing. **The gate is the subject: rusted, crooked at the top, one post bent beyond repair.** Behind him the rank coming alive — drivers, a kenkey seller, horns, someone looking up. Warm, ordinary morning light.

**Mood.** Resolution. Not a triumph — a man going home, and a town that hears him this time.

**Do not draw:** the crash (§4.2), Sarpong, an ambulance, or a hospital. This is the last picture in the book. It ends on an ordinary street.

## 7. The cover

> **DECIDED: Option B — the half-open gate.**
> Option A (the taxi at dawn) was sketched and set aside; it is the fallback if B tests poorly.
>
> Two options were sketched. Both are quiet.

**Option A — the taxi at dawn.**
Kwame's taxi on an empty road at first light. Yellow, faded, the dent in the rear passenger door visible. No driver. Dust. The road going out of frame.

**Option B — the half-open gate.**  ← **SELECTED**
The compound gate, seen from the street, half open. Nobody in the frame. Light spilling through the gap onto the compound yard beyond.

**Do not put on the cover:** a running man, a gun, a frightened woman, a police officer, a chase, blood, or a shadowy figure. This is a book about a town that learns to look — not a thriller.

**Type area.** Leave the top third clear for the title and byline: **MALLAM TOWN** · *Lecturer Sam*.

**Why B was chosen, and what that means for the drawing.** The cover and the last picture in the book are the
same object: Chapter Fifteen ends with Kwame's hand flat on this gate. The cover is therefore a promise the book
keeps on its final page — so the gate on the cover must read as the *same* gate as the one in §6.15: rusted,
crooked at the top, one post bent beyond repair. Draw them as one object, from different angles.

**Nobody is in the frame.** The cover's whole argument is that the town learns to look at what is in front of it;
putting a figure on it turns it back into a thriller. See §4.6.

## 8. Consistency checklist

Before delivery, check every drawing against the manuscript:

- [ ] Kwame's taxi has **a dent in the rear passenger door** (it matters in Ch. 5, 12 and 14)
- [ ] The **side mirror** is clearly drawn in Ch. 1, and recognisably the same mirror in Ch. 12's heap of parts
- [ ] The **phone** appears face-down in the dust in Ch. 4
- [ ] Nene's **stick** and **basket** are in every drawing she appears in
- [ ] Inspector Mensah's **cap** is under her arm, not on her head, in Ch. 13 and 14
- [ ] Mansa's **wrapper** is the same garment in Ch. 8, 12 and 14
- [ ] **Nobody is ever drawn touching Sarpong**
- [ ] No masks, skulls or shadows anywhere near Nene
- [ ] The **gate** is drawn identically in Ch. 1's world and Ch. 15

## 9. Reproduction specification

| Item | Spec |
|---|---|
| Trim size | A5 (148 × 210 mm) — confirm with the publisher |
| Image size | Full-page: **128 × 190 mm** (with margins) |
| Drawing scale | Draw at **2× final size** minimum |
| Line weight at print | 0.25 mm minimum (0.5 pt) — below this it drops out |
| File format | Vector (SVG or AI) preferred; if raster, **1200 dpi** bitmap, 1-bit |
| Colour | Black only. No CMYK, no grey. |
| Bleed | None required — all drawings sit inside the type area |
| Caption | Chapter number and title set separately; **do not letter it into the artwork** |

**Test before delivery.** Print each drawing at final size on a cheap office photocopier, then photocopy the photocopy twice. If the image still reads, it will survive a Ghanaian classroom. If it turns to mud, open the lines up.
