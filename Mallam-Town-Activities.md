# MALLAM TOWN — Discussion Questions & Activities

### for the JHS edition · by Lecturer Sam

---

## A note to the teacher

These questions are arranged in four parts, matching the four movements of the story:

| Part | Chapters | What the part is about |
|---|---|---|
| One | 1–4 | **Being blamed** — a man loses everything in one night and nobody knows it |
| Two | 5–8 | **Being believed** — a name goes on the radio, and two people believe opposite lies |
| Three | 9–12 | **Being found** — the town starts to look |
| Four | 13–15 | **Being heard** — it happens in daylight, in front of everybody |

The questions are of four kinds, and it is worth mixing them:

- **Remember** — the answer is in the book.
- **Understand** — you have to think about why.
- **Discuss** — there is more than one good answer.
- **Your turn** — about you and your own town.

Some questions are hard. They are meant to be. Let pupils disagree with each other; the argument is the lesson.

> **A caution about one thing.** Mansa is held in a locked room and is never hurt. That is a deliberate choice in this book. If a pupil asks what happened to her in there, the honest answer is: *nothing happened, and that is the point* — the town assumed otherwise, and the assumption was the cruelty. Do not invite speculation about it.

# Part One — Chapters 1 to 4

### Remember

1. What does everyone at the rank call Kwame, and why?
2. Why does Mansa use Kwame's phone instead of her own?
3. What does Kwame do when the street preacher quotes the bible?
4. What does the Old Woman on the veranda say she heard that night?
5. Why do the men on Dangerous Lane say *"Not the face"* and *"Leave his hands"*?

### Understand

6. The gate at the compound does not scream when Kwame pushes it at 11:42. At the time nobody thinks anything of it. Why does the writer tell us about it?
7. Kwame does not fight the men who take his car. *"The mathematics had never once favoured pride."* What does he mean, and do you think he was right?
8. Nene says: *"If his head were bleeding inside, no leaf would help him. That one is for the hospital, and I will not pretend otherwise."* Why is this the most important thing she says in Chapter 4?

### Discuss

9. Chapter 2 ends with Kwame driving round calling his wife's name *"at the empty roadside the way a man says a name into a room he already knows is empty."* Why does he know the room is empty?
10. Perfume is mentioned three times in the first two chapters. Why does the writer keep returning to it?

### Your turn

11. Kwame is known as a peacemaker because he settles other people's quarrels. Is there someone like that in your school or your town? What does it cost them?

# Part Two — Chapters 5 to 8

### Remember

1. How many days before Efua Nanfuri died did Kwame report his taxi stolen?
2. What does the radio say about Kwame's nickname, "The Peacemaker"?
3. Who are Kwesi and Ekow, and how old are they?
4. Where does Mansa believe Kwame went on the Monday night?

### Understand

5. When Kwame hears the broadcast, he notices something the police have not. What is it, and why does it matter so much?
6. Nene says: *"A chief inspector's daughter dies and the whole town wakes up. Two boys from the rank disappear and the town goes back to sleep."* Is she being fair to the town?
7. In Chapter 7, Nene refuses to tell Kwame whether Mansa is alive. *"Do you want me to say yes?"* Why does she refuse, when it would be kinder to lie?

### Discuss

8. Chapter 8 is told from Mansa's side. We learn that she oiled the gate, used her husband's phone, called another man, and went out at night without telling him. Do any of these things make her responsible for what happened to her?
9. Mansa thinks: *"a woman who has not been hurt is a woman who went willingly."* Where did that idea come from, and who put it there?
10. When Mansa is made to speak on the radio, *"part of her meant it."* How can a person mean something they were forced to say?

### Your turn

11. Kwame never asks Mansa where she was going that night, not even in his head. He simply believes the worst he can imagine. What should he have done?

# Part Three — Chapters 9 to 12

### Remember

1. What did Sarpong come to the compound to ask about?
2. Who is Nartey, and what does Nene find out about him?
3. What does Kwabena Dapaah do when he first sees Kwame in Nene's room?
4. How does Kwame find the door in the wall?

### Understand

5. Nene says: *"An accident happens once. This has happened twice."* What has happened twice?
6. Kwabena says: *"I did not ask."* He gives more than one reason for not asking. What are they, and which one does he believe himself?
7. When the two men close in on Kwame in the alley, Nene does not shout at them or threaten them. She says: *"I am looking for my daughter."* Why does that work better than shouting would?

### Discuss

8. The woman at the food stall sees Kwame running and looks back down into her pot. *"See nothing. Survive."* Can you blame her? At what point does looking away become the same as helping?
9. Kwame finds his own side mirror in the third house. He had polished it eleven days earlier. Why is this small thing so powerful?
10. Nene tells Kwame she will sit and wait. She does not sit and wait. Was she wrong to say it?

### Your turn

11. Nene goes door to door and asks a hundred people to simply stand in the open with their faces showing. Would you have gone? What would it have cost you?

# Part Four — Chapters 13 to 15

### Remember

1. What is the clinic record, and why is it dated the Wednesday?
2. Who is Inspector Adzo Mensah, and why has she been reading the file for three weeks?
3. What is written on the side of the toolbag?
4. What happens to Sarpong at the end?

### Understand

5. Nene says to Inspector Mensah: *"You have the paper. I have the people."* What does each woman have that the other does not?
6. Chief Inspector Nanfuri says: *"I did it because the simplest story was the easiest one to grieve with. That is not a reason."* What is he admitting?
7. In Chapter 15, not one person in a hundred puts out a hand to stop Sarpong. *"No shout. No stone. Nothing at all."* Why not?

### Discuss

8. Kwame says to Nanfuri: *"I am sorry about your daughter."* Why does he say that to the man who tried to destroy him?
9. Nene says of Sarpong: *"It is not finished for him. It is beginning. He is still a man. Somebody call the hospital."* Is she defending him? What is she actually doing?
10. The story ends: *"This time, everybody heard."* Heard what?

### Your turn

11. The crowd chants *"Peacemaker"* and Kwame answers *"No."* He says: *"I spent my whole life thinking peace meant keeping quiet. I do not believe that anymore."* Is there a difference between keeping the peace and keeping quiet? Give an example from your own school.

# The whole book

1. Three women carry this story: Nene, Mansa and Inspector Adzo Mensah. What does each one know that the men do not?
2. The book is full of people who saw something and said nothing — the woman at the food stall, the boy who laughed, Kwabena Dapaah, half the town. Pick one and argue either that you understand them or that you do not.
3. Kwame is cleared because of a police ledger, a clinic record and a nurse's note. What does that say about what counts as proof?
4. Nobody in this book is rescued by a clever detective. Who actually solves it, and how?
5. Money does a lot of damage in this story: Sarpong pays Kwabena half, Nartey takes money to carry messages, Kwabena had three children in school. What is the book saying about being paid to not ask questions?
6. Kwame and Mansa are not reconciled at the end. The book says *"it was beginning — which was the thing that mattered."* Why is that a better ending than a happy one?
7. The gate bookends the novel: silent in Chapter 1, screaming in Chapter 15. What changed?
8. Who is braver — Nene, who kneels down beside the man who did this, or Kwame, who walks out in front of a hundred people and a police chief? Argue for one.
9. *"Everything in this town happens in the dark,"* says Nene. *"Let us have this one in the light, for once."* Where else in the book does the dark do the work that daylight would not allow?
10. If Mallam Town had one habit to unlearn, which one would the book say it is?

# Who said these words?

Ten lines from the book. Write down who says each one, and to whom. Answers are at the bottom of the page.

1. *"I have heard nothing tonight. Not even the gate."*
2. *"You're the husband."*
3. *"I built the door."*
4. *"I am not here to arrest anyone."*
5. *"Suspicion is not proof."*
6. *"I did not go. They made me say it on the radio."*
7. *"I am looking for my daughter."*
8. *"You have the paper. I have the people."*
9. *"That is not a reason."*
10. *"He is still a man. Somebody call the hospital."*

### Answers

1. The Old Woman on the veranda, to Kwame.
2. Kwabena Dapaah, when he sees Kwame in Nene's room.
3. Kwabena Dapaah, to Nene and Kwame.
4. Inspector Adzo Mensah, at Nene's door.
5. Inspector Adzo Mensah, to Nene.
6. Mansa, to Kwame, in the room where she was held.
7. Nene, to the two men closing in on Kwame.
8. Nene, to Inspector Adzo Mensah.
9. Chief Inspector Nanfuri, to Kwame.
10. Nene, over Sarpong where he lies on the track.

# Things to write

1. **The radio broadcast.** Write the correction that Chief Inspector Nanfuri has to read out on every station that carried Kwame's name. Make it sound like him: official, careful, and ashamed.
2. **Ama's morning.** Ama at the MTN kiosk has a bad back and a boy who was treated for worms and never paid for. Write the scene where Nene comes to ask her about the number. What does Ama want in return?
3. **The three nights.** Chapter 11 tells us that Kwabena planed the same piece of wood for three nights. Write the second night from inside his head. Do not let him say the word *door*.
4. **Maame Adjoa.** Write the letter she takes to the police station after Chapter 14, about her son Kwesi. She has had a year to imagine writing it.
5. **The Old Woman.** She has not walked past her own gate in two years, and she walks to the uncompleted houses in Chapter 14. Write her reason, in her own words.
6. **One year later.** Write a page set a year after the last chapter. Choose one: Kwame and Mansa in the compound; Kwabena at his bench; Inspector Adzo Mensah at the police post; or Sarpong in a hospital bed.
7. **A different ending for Chapter 12.** What if Nene had sat and waited, as she said she would? Rewrite the last scene of Chapter 12 so that Kwame does not come back.

# Things to do

1. **Map it.** Draw a map of Mallam Town from the clues in the book: the rank, the compound house, Dangerous Lane, the cemetery, the uncompleted houses, Nene's compound, the police post, the MTN kiosk. Put a mark everywhere something important happens. Then check: **how many uncompleted houses are there?** Go back through the book and find out. You will discover that the story never actually says — it only ever names *the third house*. Decide how many to draw, and be ready to argue for your number.
2. **The timeline.** Make a wall timeline of the whole story, Monday night to Friday dawn. Use only what the book tells you — and nothing else. Then find the two events that are four days apart. That gap is the key to the whole book; almost every reader misses it on the first read. Say why it proves Kwame could not have done it.
3. **The day names.** Every major character is named for the day of the week they were born. Work out the day for each one, then find out the day-name of somebody in your own class and write a paragraph about them.
4. **A notice for the rank.** In Chapter 6 the radio tells the public not to approach Kwame. Design the notice the police would have pinned up at the rank. Then design the notice they should have pinned up afterwards.
5. **Role play.** Take the scene in Chapter 14 where Mansa tells the truth in front of the town. Cast: Mansa, Inspector Adzo Mensah (taking notes), Kwame, Nanfuri, Nene, Papa Kofi. Run it twice — once with Mansa interrupted, once with her allowed to finish. Discuss the difference.
6. **The illustration brief.** The artist has to draw one picture per chapter, fifteen in all. Choose your chapter and describe exactly what should be in the frame, what should be left out, and why. (There is a full plan in the illustration notes.)
