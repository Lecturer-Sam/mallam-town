# MALLAM TOWN — Design Specification

### How the book should look, and why

There is a visual mockup alongside this document: **`Mallam-Town-Design-Mockup.html`**. Open that first — it shows the two covers, the grid, a type specimen, a chapter opening, a text spread and a glossary page, all at true proportions.

---

## The short answer

> **A5 · 96 pages · perfect bound · black only inside · black plus one warm ochre on the cover · set in Georgia at 11.5 on 16.**

Every one of those is a cost decision as much as a design decision. Here is the reasoning.

---

## 1. Trim size — A5 (148 × 210 mm)  ·  **LOCKED**

A5 is the standard for a Ghanaian supplementary reader. It fits a school bag, it is the cheapest common format, and at roughly 15,300 words it lands at about **96 pages** — substantial enough to feel like a real book to a thirteen-year-old, short enough to finish in a term.

**B5** (176 × 250 mm) would give the illustrations more room and more presence, and would drop the page count to about 60. It costs more per copy. **A4** is a textbook size, not a reader size, and a 70-page A4 book looks thin and institutional.

**If you have a publisher with a house format, use theirs** — the consistency across a list is worth more than my preference.

---

> **Extent corrected after the full book was assembled.** The original estimate was 80 pp. Building every
> page — 15,453 words of story, ~1,600 of front matter, ~4,300 of back matter, fifteen illustration panels,
> and the blanks that chapter-recto starts force — runs to **122 pages in the mockup**, and a typesetter
> packing pages to their full capacity would land near **96**. Budget for **96 pp**, and recompute the spine
> from the printer's bulking dummy rather than from this number.

## 2. Paper and binding

| | |
|---|---|
| **Text** | 80 gsm **cream** offset. Cream rather than bright white: less glare under a classroom bulb, and it hides show-through at this weight. |
| **Endpapers** | 120 gsm, carrying the map |
| **Cover** | 300 gsm art card, **matt** laminate — gloss scuffs visibly by the end of a term |
| **Binding** | **Perfect bound**, PUR if the printer has it |

**On binding, a real trade-off.** At 96 pages, perfect binding is right: it gives a square spine you can letter, so the book is findable on a shelf. But if the page count comes in under 64, **saddle-stitch is cheaper and lies flatter** — and a book that lies flat is easier to read at a desk. Section-sewn is the most durable by a distance and almost certainly too expensive. If this is a class set that will be reissued yearly, ask the printer to quote section-sewn before deciding.

Pad the extent to a **multiple of 8**.

---

## 3. Colour — the one place to spend money

**Interior: black only.** Keeping the inside one-colour is the difference between an affordable book and an unaffordable one, and the line art was designed for exactly that.

**Cover: black plus one spot colour.** The accent is a faded ochre — **#B4811F** — which is the yellow of Kwame's taxi, "faded by the sun". It is the book's central object and its most recognisable colour, so the cover and the story are keyed to the same thing. It carries the byline, the rule, the chapter numbers and the drop caps, and nothing else.

Two spot colours on a cover cost very little over one. Two colours throughout the interior would roughly double the print bill.

---

## 4. Typography

**Georgia throughout.** A large x-height, sturdy serifs, and it holds up at small sizes. Specifically **not** Times New Roman: its x-height is too small for a struggling thirteen-year-old, which is exactly who this book needs to work for. If your printer has a licensed text face with a generous x-height — Minion, Utopia, Adobe Caslon — use it; otherwise Georgia is genuinely the right call.

| Element | Spec |
|---|---|
| Body | **11.5 pt on 16 pt** — go 12/17 if there are struggling readers |
| Measure | **58 characters** — inside the 55–65 comfort band |
| Chapter title | Georgia 29 pt |
| Chapter number | Verdana small caps 10.5 pt, letter-spaced, ochre |
| Running head | Verdana 10.5 pt — book title verso, chapter recto |
| Folio | Verdana 11.5 pt, foot, outer margin |
| Glossary & questions | Georgia 10 pt on 14 pt |
| Paragraphs | **Indented first line, no space between** |
| Hyphenation | **Off** — ragged right is fine; rivers are worse |

Two of those are worth defending.

**Indented paragraphs, not spaced.** Block paragraphs with space between are common in school books and easier for very weak readers to track. But this is a novel, and a thirteen-year-old who has just read eighty pages should feel they have read a *book*, not a worksheet. Indent, and compensate with generous leading.

**No hyphenation.** At A5 with a 58-character measure, disabling hyphenation costs you a slightly ragged right edge and buys you the absence of rivers. Take that trade.

---

## 5. The grid

| | |
|---|---|
| Head | 20 mm |
| Foot | 18 mm |
| Outer (fore-edge) | 18 mm |
| **Inner (binding)** | **24 mm** |
| Type area | **106 × 172 mm** |
| Lines per page | ~42 |

The inner margin is 6 mm deeper than the outer because perfect binding eats 6–8 mm into the gutter. Get this wrong and the innermost characters disappear into the spine — the single most common fault in cheaply produced paperbacks.

**Single column, always.** At A5, two columns make the measure too short and the page starts to look like a newspaper.

---

## 6. Illustrations

**One drawing at the head of each chapter**, 106 mm wide × 62 mm tall — the full width of the type area — with the chapter number, title and rule beneath it.

**The alternative is a full-page plate facing each chapter opening.** It is more beautiful, and if the budget allows it, it is the upgrade I would take first. But it adds fifteen pages, and at this age the picture does more work *next to the words it opens* than across the gutter from them. The half-page panel keeps the book at 96 pages and keeps the visual cue exactly where a struggling reader needs it.

**One ornament only: a three-line drop cap in ochre** on chapter openings. Nothing else. No decorative rules, no chapter ornaments, no historiated initials. The art is the ornament.

---

## 7. The cover

Two options were drawn. Both are quiet, and both deliberately refuse the thriller treatment — no running man, no gun, no shadowy figure, no frightened woman.

- **Option A — the taxi at dawn.** The battered taxi alone on an empty road, with the unfinished buildings on the horizon. Lonelier, and it puts the book's central object on the front.
- **Option B — the half-open gate.** The gate standing open onto an empty compound yard. More mysterious, and it is the image the book ends on.

> **DECIDED — Option B, the half-open gate.** The author chose B over A. B promises a question rather than a
> setting, and it pays off the last image in the book: Chapter Fifteen ends on Kwame's hand flat on that same
> gate, so the cover and the final page are the same object. Option A (the taxi at dawn) is the safer
> commercial cover — a taxi reads instantly as Ghanaian to a parent or a bookseller — and it remains the
> fallback if B tests poorly.
>
> **Note for the printer, since B was chosen:** the gate is a single focal object with a lot of fine rust and
> corrugation detail. On matt laminate that detail is the first thing to fill in. Ask to see it wet-proofed at
> 100% before the run, and be ready to open the line work up slightly.

| | |
|---|---|
| Title | Georgia, upper case, black, upper third |
| Byline | **LECTURER SAM**, Verdana caps, letter-spaced, ochre |
| Art | Lower 61%, full bleed to three edges |
| Foot | Publisher line, reversed out of the art |
| Spine | ~6 mm at 96 pp — title and byline at 7 pt, running top to bottom |
| Back | Blurb (drafted in the mockup), author note with photograph, barcode with 12 mm quiet space |

---

## 8. Sequence

**Front matter** — half title · (epigraph, optional, on the verso) · title · copyright · dedication · note on this edition · contents · the people in this book · a note on the names

**Text** — chapters 1–15, each opening with its illustration

**Back matter** — glossary · discussion questions · things to write · things to do · about the author · one blank leaf

**Endpapers** — the map, split across the fold

> **The last thing in the book should be the map.** The reader closes it having just walked the whole town.

---

## 9. What to send the printer

| | |
|---|---|
| Text | PDF/X-1a, CMYK, but **black channel only** — no rich black in the interior |
| Cover | PDF/X-1a, CMYK, **two spot colours** (black + the ochre as a spot, not a build) |
| Images | 1200 dpi 1-bit bitmap, or vector |
| Bleed | 3 mm on the cover; none on the interior |
| Extent | **96 pp** (multiple of 8) + 4 pp cover |
| Proof | **Wet proof the cover.** A litho proof will not show you what the ochre actually does on matt laminate. |

**One instruction worth putting in writing:** ask for a **bulking dummy** if the page count is still moving. Eighty pages of 80 gsm and eighty pages of 70 gsm are visibly different books, and the spine width — and therefore the spine type — depends on it.

---

## 10. The two decisions only you can make

1. ✅ **Cover: Option B — the half-open gate.** Decided. A is the fallback.
2. ✅ **Format: A5 (148 × 210 mm).** Decided — not a house format.

Everything else here will hold.
